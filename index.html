      Staying on the right side of the law

      What the cameras catch, and what to do instead

      • Red robotStop behind the white stop line and wait for green. Treat amber as stop unless braking would be unsafe. Police say red-light cases are likely to go to court instead of a fine.
      • Blocking the junctionDo not stop on the pedestrian crossing or inside the intersection. Enter only when your exit is clear.
      • Phone in handHolding or using a phone while driving is an offence. Set your route and this app before you move.
      • SeatbeltsDriver and passengers buckle up before the car moves.
      • Wrong side of the roadNever jump the queue by driving against oncoming traffic. Stay in your lane and turn only from the correct one.
      • SpeedKeep to the posted limit. In town that is generally 60 km/h unless a sign says lower.
      • Picking up and dropping offUse designated stops and ranks, not the junction.
      • Officer on point dutyFollow the officer's signals even against the light. Police have said you will not be penalised for doing so.
      • On footCross at marked crossings. Police say pedestrians are being watched too.

      If a notice arrives

      • How it reaches youAn SMS to the registered owner with a video clip and the fine. Officers review each clip before a ticket goes out.
      • Where to checkOnly at paymyfines.co.zw or paymyfines.gov.zw, or at a police traffic station such as Harare Central, Avondale, Warren Park, Milton Park, Marlborough, Mabelreign, Mbare or Chitungwiza. Treat any other link as a scam.
      • What it costsMost of these offences carry the standard US$30 deposit fine, payable in ZiG. Check the notice for your exact amount.
      • Do not ignore itPolice warn that unanswered tickets can lead to blacklisting and a warrant of arrest.
      • Sold your car?Change ownership within two weeks, or the new driver's tickets come to you.
